# Narrative frame — the room changes as you read

## Selected direction

On 10 September 2026, the user selected the first narrative concept: a personal room that changes as the reader moves through memories and discoveries. They asked to combine it with the second concept's books-and-posters arrangement for displaying authors' work in each bridge.

The room supplies continuity across the personal narrative. The studio-like arrangement supplies the bridges' display language. This is a narrative decision, not an implementation specification or approval of new site code.

## Proposed traversal

1. Enter the present room and approach a familiar book.
2. Enter the childhood-reading vignette through that object. An illustrated composite can carry memory without claiming to reconstruct a historical room exactly.
3. Return to the room for Bridge A. The bookcase and adjacent wall become a display of works and the categories under which they have been received.
4. A book or printed page carries the reader into the college/translation vignette.
5. Return for Bridge B. The desk and wall show works alongside the translators, editors and publications involved in their circulation.
6. The genre-line vignette questions how the accumulated books can be arranged.
7. End at the original room view, with discoveries still present and research gaps made legible.

These beats elaborate the selected combination; their exact scenes and gestures remain open to iteration.

## Bridge A — books and their labels

Use a close view of the bookcase and nearby wall. Each existing classification mode is a small composition: a work in the foreground, the writer's name, and a relevant publication image or explanatory slip behind it.

Focus on one cluster at a time. Pulling forward a work or lifting a label reveals the short case. Further reading opens from that case. Keep the connection to the preceding Zhang Xiaofeng vignette visible through its initiating book or note.

## Bridge B — works and the people who carry them

Use the desk for comparing an original publication with a translated edition or anthology. Use the wall above it for credited translators, editors, magazines and presses.

The composition changes with each gateway mode: an edition pair for translation, an anthology and contents for editing, a group of publications for a press. State the role of every depicted connection and base it on the cited record.

## Continuity

- Return to a recognizable room view between the personal scenes and the bridges.
- Leave selected books and notes in place after each bridge, so the room accumulates the reading.
- Let the reader's small actions reveal narrative information: pick up, turn, compare, unfold, place back.
- Keep a clear next story beat even when a display allows optional exploration.
- Preserve space for ordinary personal objects, making this recognizably the narrator's room.
- Explain an empty place when it represents a specific research gap.

## Alternatives explored

The standalone studio investigation, bookstore journey and impossible room interior remain earlier alternatives. The accepted combination uses the changing-room narrative and the studio's display arrangements; it does not adopt those alternatives as the site's overall plot.
